### Impact

#### An Augmented Reality (AR) mirror device aimed at enhancing the online shopping experience for individuals with mobility challenges.

The AR Fitting Room enables users to virtually try on clothing and accessories from the comfort of their homes, significantly reducing the need for offline store visits.

![[overview.png]]

### Background

#### Inspired by my grandfather's struggle with mobility, I sought an inclusive shopping experience without physical barriers.

Everything started with my grandfather. He loved to be fashionable, but as Parkinson's disease affected his mobility, trips to the mall became exhausting. The lack of accessibility was a barrier, but the most heartbreaking part was watching him physically struggle to try on clothes multiple times just to find the right fit. That experience planted a seed: Shopping should be an enjoyable experience for everyone, regardless of their physical condition.

### The Problem

#### While digital alternatives remove physical travel, traditional e-commerce introduces frustrating "fit uncertainty."

People with mobility issues face significant hurdles offline, driving 50% of individuals with disabilities to shop online weekly (compared to just 22% of general consumers). While accessibility is their #1 deciding factor for choosing an e-commerce platform, traditional online shopping fails them by creating "fit uncertainty." Users cannot physically try items on through a small screen, leading to purchase hesitation and high return rates.

### The Solution Sketch

#### An AR-powered full-body mirror that brings the fitting room to the user's home, eliminating physical exertion.
What if users could try on clothes in their own room?

![Solution sketch](/arfittingroom/sketch.png)

How can I create an inclusive shopping experience that addresses accessibility challenges, such as finding the right fit without visiting a physical store or trying on clothes multiple times?

A full-body mirror with a built-in camera can provide the best online shopping service.

This potential solution offers an AR fitting room service, allowing users to try on clothes in the comfort of their own space. The AR feature will allow users to try on clothes virtually. Instead of traveling to a store, users can browse and virtually wear 3D garments mapped to their real-time body data right in their own rooms — complete with recording and sharing options to get a "second opinion" from friends.

### The Challenge

#### Designing for a massive mirror interface required stepping away from digital screens to build a real-size physical prototype.

Designing for a full-length mirror is fundamentally different from designing for a mobile phone or monitor. Treating design elements intended for smaller devices would result in an interface too large and awkward for practical use. To solve this, I stepped away from Figma and created a **real-size paper wireframe**. This physical prototyping was crucial for accurately measuring and testing ergonomic sizes for icons and buttons on a human scale.

![Paper wireframe prototype](/arfittingroom/challenge.png)

### User Testing & Iterations

#### Testing in physical space revealed critical ergonomic pain points, leading to dynamic, accessible UI adjustments.

Because this device relies on physical interaction, user testing drove three critical accessibility pivots:

- **Left-Handed Accessibility:** A left-handed user found the right-aligned nav bar hard to reach. I introduced an initial pre-setting stage allowing users to choose the navigation bar's primary location.

- **Height Variations & Wheelchairs:** A fixed menu doesn't accommodate varying user heights or people in wheelchairs. I designed a flexible menu bar that users can freely swipe and relocate anywhere on the screen via a long-press interaction, instantly adapting to their current posture.

- **The "Selfie Distance" Problem:** Because the camera is at the top of a massive mirror, users couldn't tap "shoot" and capture their full outfit simultaneously. I implemented a camera timer, allowing users to tap, step back, and strike a proper pose.


### Unpacking The Solution
#### Familiar, kiosk-inspired simplicity designed to eliminate new-device anxiety.

Introducing a massive, novel smart mirror into someone's home could easily overwhelm them. To bridge this gap, I drew inspiration from the familiar digital kiosks found in physical shopping malls. By keeping the interface intentionally minimal—relying on straightforward buttons and a clean layout—the mirror feels instantly approachable. The goal was to ensure users feel entirely in control, making the tech feel like an intuitive helper rather than an intimidating machine.

### Pre-Setting Stage

#### The mirror adapts to the user, offering personalized ergonomic calibrations before the journey begins.

True accessibility starts before a single item of clothing is browsed. Before stepping into the AR experience, users are greeted with a quick, customizable setup phase. Here, they can calibrate the interface to their specific physical conditions, locking in their preferred hand dominance and adjusting the base height of the controls. The device molds to the user, not the other way around.

### Let's Do Shopping

#### A seamless e-commerce journey that physically bends to your posture in real-time.

At its core, navigating the mirror feels as fluid and familiar as browsing your favorite store on a laptop or phone. Users can seamlessly select garments, build a wishlist, and toggle the AR try-on feature. But the real magic lies in its physical flexibility: if a user starts out standing but suddenly needs to sit down, they aren't trapped by their initial settings. By simply long-pressing the navigation bar, they can drag and relocate it to a lower, comfortable position without ever breaking their shopping flow or returning to a settings menu.

### Experience AR Fitting Room

#### Virtual try-ons, contextual backgrounds, and the classic "fitting room selfie"—all from home.

This is where the physical and digital worlds seamlessly merge. Once a garment is selected, the mirror's camera maps the 3D clothing directly onto the user's real-time reflection, allowing them to twist, pose, and genuinely check the fit as if they were wearing it. Want to know how that jacket looks outdoors? Users can swipe through virtual backgrounds, much like a video call filter, to visualize the outfit in different environments. Finally, bringing back the joy of shopping with friends, a built-in timer lets users step back, strike a pose, and instantly share the captured photos or videos with their loved ones for that much-needed second opinion.

### Takeaways

#### Designing for physical accessibility taught me that real-world testing is the ultimate compass for solving digital-physical pain points.

This solo project reinforced that trying different methods helps in reaching a solution. Designing for an everyday physical product required a deep understanding of physical space—factors like user height, preferred hand, and gaze direction became just as important as pixel perfection. Stepping outside the digital realm to utilize physical paper wireframes helped me identify real-world pain points and deliver a genuinely inclusive design.
